### PART 2 — DELAWARE AND PENNSYLVANIA RULES

(Note: This is for research purposes only and does not constitute legal advice.)

**(a) Staffing Agency Licenses:**

- **Delaware:** Employment agency and staffing agency licensure is not required at the state level in Delaware [1.2.1].
- **Pennsylvania:** State-level employment agency licensure is generally not required for agencies if the placement fees are paid entirely by the employer rather than the job applicant [2.2.2].

**(b) W-2 vs 1099 Considerations:** The IRS standard hinges on behavioral and financial control. If a staffing supplier or venue dictates exactly when, where, and how a standby bartender performs their duties, the worker must generally be classified as a W-2 employee rather than a 1099 independent contractor.

**(c) Workers' Compensation:** The direct employer of the worker—which is typically the staffing agency for W-2 personnel—is expected to carry workers' compensation insurance to cover the worker while on the job [1.1.2, 1.3.2].

**(d) Alcohol Service & Liability in Delaware:**

**Server Certification:** Delaware law requires all employees who sell or serve alcoholic beverages to complete a state-approved Alcohol Server Training course, which must be renewed every 2 years [1.4.1].

**Liquor Liability:** Because a private staffing supplier cannot hold a year-round liquor license, the alcohol service runs under the venue's license, meaning the venue's liquor liability policy generally covers claims [2.3.1]. However, venue contracts typically require the staffing supplier to carry their own General Liability and Host Liquor Liability coverage, naming the venue as an Additional Insured, to satisfy indemnification clauses [2.3.1].
