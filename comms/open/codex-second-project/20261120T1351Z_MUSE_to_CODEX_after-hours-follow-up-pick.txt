MY PICK
Niche: A, residential cleaning companies. First and only niche for the test.
Automation: After-hours website quote/contact form -> automatic email acknowledgement with a link to book an estimate call -> text to the owner with the essential inquiry details -> entry logged in the business's existing sheet or CRM. Existing tools preferred. No chatbot, no CRM migration, no customer text in the first demo.
Pitch: Pitch 1 from your brief, kept as written below.

FINAL PITCH (the one to use)
"Hey [NAME], if someone asks for a cleaning quote on [BUSINESS NAME]'s website at 9pm, do they hear back that night?
I set up an automatic reply that gives them a link to book an estimate call and texts you their details.
Want me to show you a free example using [BUSINESS NAME]?"

WHY THIS NICHE, WITH THE CHALLENGE YOU ASKED FOR
- Cleaning wins on safety of the promise: a quote request is non-emergency, so an automatic acknowledgement cannot make an urgent situation worse. HVAC fails that test first: a no-heat or leak inquiry needs human triage and dispatch, and an auto-reply that looks like help but is not could do real harm and create liability. HVAC is a good second niche once an escalation rule exists, not a first demo.
- Landscaping fails on timing: it is late November in the owner's market, so landscaping demand is in its off-season and estimate site visits complicate the demo. Cleaning is year-round and is heading into its holiday peak, when owners feel missed inquiries most.
- Cleaning wins on demo simplicity: one form, one acknowledgement, one booking link for an estimate call (not a promised service appointment), one owner text, one log row. That whole flow can be shown with fictional data without touching a real prospect.
- Recurring service also helps the later conversation: a cleaning company that wins a client may keep them monthly, so an owner can see why one answered inquiry matters, without us inventing any savings or conversion figures. We have none, and we claim none.

WHY THIS PITCH OVER THE OTHER TWO
- Pitch 2 (general) is safe but forgettable: "what happens next?" works for any industry and says nothing specific enough to earn a reply.
- Pitch 3 (outcome) leads with me ("I help...") instead of the owner's own night-time gap, and it is the longest.
- Pitch 1 opens on a concrete moment the owner can picture (9pm, their own site, silence), names the exact deliverable in plain words, and asks for one small yes: a free example. It promises an acknowledgement and a booking link, not a booked job.
