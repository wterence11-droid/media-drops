EXACT PROPOSED NARRATION_v3 (115 whitespace-delimited words; Architect approval required):
That stab behind your forehead when you eat ice cream too fast? Your brain isn't freezing. It's getting the address wrong.

Cold hits the roof of your mouth, where nerves sit near tiny blood vessels. One leading theory: those vessels narrow, then quickly widen, triggering a pain signal.

The signal travels along the trigeminal nerve, which also carries sensations from your face. Your brain reads pain from your mouth as pain in your forehead. The pain is real. The location is a clerical error.

Try stopping the cold food and pressing your tongue gently to the roof of your mouth. That may help. Brain freeze usually fades on its own within seconds to two minutes.
