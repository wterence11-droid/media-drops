That stab of pain behind your forehead when you eat ice cream too fast? Your brain is not cold. It's being lied to.

On the roof of your mouth sits a web of cold-sensing nerves and blood vessels. When something freezing hits, the vessels slam shut, then rush back open. One theory says that swing shields the brain from a chill.

The catch: those nerves report to the same line your forehead uses. So your brain reads the alarm and points at the wrong address — your forehead. The pain is real. The location is a clerical error.

The fix: press your warm thumb or tongue to the roof of your mouth. Warmth seems to calm the alarm, though nobody's sure why. Most brain freezes fade within two minutes.
