## Concept
**"Brain freeze is your body lying to you"** — what actually happens inside your head when you eat ice cream too fast, and the 10-second fix.

Why this one: universally experienced (instant recognition in frame 1), genuinely visual in cross-section (throat → nerve bundle → forehead), a real mechanism that is simple AND accurate, and it ends with a useful payoff (the fix) instead of trivia. Topic is swappable at the owner's word without changing the test design.

## Narration script (~45 seconds, ~110 words)
> "That stab of pain behind your forehead when you eat ice cream too fast? Your brain is not cold. It's being lied to.
>
> At the back of your throat sits a bundle of nerves whose day job is guarding the blood vessels that feed your brain. When something freezing hits it, it slams those vessels shut — protecting the brain from a sudden chill.
>
> The catch: that nerve bundle reports to the same line your forehead uses. So your brain reads the alarm and points at the wrong address — your forehead. The pain is real. The location is a clerical error.
>
> The fix: press your warm thumb to the roof of your mouth. Warm the guard post, the alarm stops, the vessels reopen. Ten seconds."

## Visual beats (coded animation, D-1109-1 route — programmatic default, image-led hook)
1. **0–2s (hook, image-led):** extreme close cross-section — ice cream hitting the back of a throat, frost bloom. First frame must read muted: pain incoming.
2. **2–10s:** pull back to the nerve bundle at the throat; it flashes alarm-red as the cold registers.
3. **10–22s:** camera travels up; brain blood vessels visibly narrow; a "temperature guard" gauge slams down.
4. **22–33s:** signal line splits — signal runs to FOREHEAD and lights it up while the throat source pulses. On-screen caption: "real pain. wrong address."
5. **33–45s:** warm thumb presses the palate; alarm fades, vessels reopen, color returns. End card: the fix, one line.
Look: clean medical-illustration style, deep blue/teal palette with red alarm accents — our own art direction, not any existing creator's palette or character design.

## Accuracy notes (checked against the real mechanism)
- The nerve bundle is the **sphenopalatine ganglion**, behind the nose/throat. Cold triggers rapid constriction then dilation of brain-feeding vessels; the referred pain is felt in the forehead because the trigeminal nerve carries both signals. Script says "back of your throat" as plain-English placement — acceptable simplification, mechanism is right.
- Thumb-to-palate warming is a commonly recommended remedy and matches the mechanism (warm the palate → signal resets).
