# AFTER THE BEEP
Synopsis, Episodes 1 to 5

**Genre:** Supernatural thriller, grief mystery

**Logline:** At her husband's funeral reception, Mara's phone rings from his dead number. His voicemails warn her about tomorrow, and tomorrow keeps coming true, until the night a man with his face knocks on her door and his voice begs her not to open it.

## Main characters
- **Mara Voss, 34.** The widow. Controlled, dry-eyed, fraying. She wears Daniel's ring on a chain. She wants one more minute with him, and that want is the lever the story pulls.
- **Daniel Voss.** Her husband, dead three days when the story opens. Heard as a warm, calm voice on the phone. Seen only at the door, younger than when he died.
- **June, 29.** Mara's younger sister. Practical and worried. She says what the audience is thinking.
- **Reyes.** Daniel's brother, a detective. He believes in records, and the records keep failing him.

## Overall story
**Beginning.** A dead man's number calls his widow with warnings about tomorrow. Each one comes true.
**Main conflict.** The warnings are not free. Every time Mara obeys, the harm lands on someone else, and something wearing Daniel's face wants to be let in. Mara has to work out who is really on the line and what it costs to keep listening.
**Where it is headed.** The messages are a loop. Someone recorded them to save Mara, and the trail leads back to her own hand. Saving her sister means sending the first message herself.

**Free and paid split:** Episodes 1, 2 and 3 are free. Episodes 4 and 5 are paid.

## Episode 1: After the Beep
**Episode type:** Free

**Opening scene hook:** At the funeral reception, Mara's phone lights up on the table. The caller ID is Daniel's photo. She whispers, "That's Daniel's phone."

Mara answers. Daniel's voice, calm and gentle, tells her not to take the elevator tomorrow at 8:04. She tells her sister June it was a wrong number. Daniel's brother Reyes, a detective, checks the line: the number has been dead since the accident. That night a voicemail arrives without a ring. The voice talks as if tomorrow has already happened, and adds a second warning: Thursday, 9 PM, do not open the door, because whatever speaks to her will not be him. The next morning Mara takes the stairs. Below her, the elevator falls. She tests the voice with a question only Daniel could answer. It answers, and adds something she never told anyone. Her fear turns into grief. She starts to believe.

**Episode ending:** Thursday, 9 PM. Three slow knocks. Through the peephole stands Daniel, younger, smiling the smile from their wedding photos, asking her to open up. In her other hand the voicemail is still playing: Daniel's real voice, terrified, telling her not to. Her fingers touch the lock.

## Episode 2: The Man at the Door
**Episode type:** Free

Mara does not open the door. The man outside does not leave. He talks to her through the wood all night, in Daniel's voice, with Daniel's memories, and each time she looks through the peephole he is older. By dawn he looks the age Daniel was when he died. Then the hallway is empty. June arrives, finds her sister on the floor against the door, and decides Mara is breaking down. Reyes pulls the building's hallway camera. It shows the door, and Mara's shadow under it, and nobody outside. But the phone company gives him something he cannot explain: every one of the calls was routed through a cell tower that was taken down in 2019, the year Mara and Daniel met. A new voicemail arrives. The voice says the man was early, that he will come back, and that she kept him out yesterday. Mara needs to hear Daniel say anything else. She calls his number to listen to his old greeting.

**Episode ending:** The greeting plays. It is not Daniel's voice. It is Mara's own voice, saying his words: "You've reached Daniel. Leave it after the beep."

## Episode 3: The Price of a Warning
**Episode type:** Free (last free episode)

The warnings keep coming, and Mara keeps obeying. One tells her not to cross at the corner at noon. She waits. A van takes the corner too fast and hits the man who stepped out in her place. She begins to keep a list, and the list is ugly: every warning she obeyed, and the stranger who was hurt in the same minute. The harm does not go away. It moves. Reyes, studying the funeral home's camera footage, finds a mourner at the back of Daniel's service that no one in the family can name. The face never turns to the lens. Then the next voicemail comes: Friday, do not answer when June calls. Friday comes. June calls. Mara stares at her sister's name and cannot do it. She answers. The line is open and silent. June never arrives for dinner.

**Episode ending (paywall hook):** Reyes freezes one frame of the funeral footage. The stranger at the back is the man from the door, standing right behind June. Mara's phone rings. The caller ID says June. The voice that answers is not June's. It is the man from the door: "You should have opened it, Mara."

## Episode 4: Storage Unit 9
**Episode type:** Paid

June is gone, and the man with Daniel's face has her phone. Reyes stops arguing with what he has seen. In Daniel's bank records he finds a storage unit, paid in cash every month, with one payment made after the funeral. Mara and Reyes cut the lock on Unit 9. Inside there is a folding table, a chair, and Daniel's phone, plugged in and fully charged, the phone that was supposed to have been destroyed in the accident. Beside it sits a second phone Mara has never seen. It holds a single unsent draft, dated tomorrow. She reads it. It is every warning she has received, word for word, in order, ending with one she has not heard yet: where June is, and what it will cost to get her back. On the table is Mara's own handwriting, in a notebook she does not remember filling.

**Episode ending:** Daniel's phone rings in her hand. The caller ID does not say Daniel. It says MARA.

## Episode 5: First Message
**Episode type:** Paid

Mara answers the call from her own name and hears breathing she knows. The notebook explains the rest. The line only carries a warning backward if someone pays for it going forward, and it speaks in the one voice the listener will obey. Mara would never have believed herself. She would have believed Daniel. The man at the door is the collector, wearing the face that would get him let in. To bring June back, Mara has to begin what she has been living through. In Unit 9 she picks up the second phone and records the first message, the one that started everything: tomorrow, 8:04, don't take the elevator. She says it gently, the way he would have. The loop closes. Her phone lights up with June's name, and this time it is June, safe, annoyed, asking why Mara is crying.

**Episode ending:** Mara walks out of the storage unit into daylight. Her phone rings from an unknown number. She answers. It is her own voice, years older and very tired: "You missed one."
