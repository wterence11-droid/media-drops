TITLE PLAQUE: Transfer-on-death deed: 3 catches

NARRATION (also the burned-in captions, word for word)
1. A transfer-on-death deed names who gets your house when you die. That house passes outside probate. But there are three catches.
2. One. It has to be recorded with the county before you die. In Texas and Utah, a signed deed that was never recorded does not work.
3. Two. Your debts can follow the house. If your estate cannot pay what it owes, the estate can go after that property. Utah allows twelve months to start. Hawaii allows eighteen.
4. Three. In Texas, a will cannot change it. To undo the deed, you record a new one, or a written cancellation, before you die.
5. So check three things. Is this deed allowed in your state? Is it recorded? Does it still name the right person? This is general information, not legal advice.

WHERE EACH FACT COMES FROM (read Tue Nov 17 2026)
- Recorded before death: Utah Code 75-6-409 (a TOD deed shall "be recorded before the transferor's death in the public records" of the county recorder where the property is). https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title75/Chapter6/C75-6-P4_2019051420190514.pdf . Texas Estates Code ch. 114 (to be effective the deed must be recorded before the transferor's death in the county clerk's deed records). https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/ES/pdf/ES.114.pdf
- Debts / creditor reach: Hawaii Rev. Stat. 527-15: to the extent the probate estate is insufficient to satisfy an allowed claim, the estate may enforce the liability against property transferred by a TOD deed; proceeding not later than eighteen months after death. https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol12_Ch0501-0588/HRS0527/HRS_0527-0015.htm . Utah Code 75-6-415: same rule, 12 months.
- A will cannot change it (Texas): Tex. Estates Code ch. 114: "A will may not revoke or supersede a transfer on death deed"; revocation is by a later TOD deed or an instrument of revocation, acknowledged and recorded before death. https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/84R/billtext/html/SB00462F.htm
- "Passes outside probate": the purpose of the uniform act (non-probate transfer at death).
